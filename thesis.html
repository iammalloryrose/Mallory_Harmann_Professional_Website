Master's Thesis · Texas A&M University

A GIS-Based Assessment of Socio-Hydrologic Vulnerability in Census Tracts of El Paso County, Texas

Mallory Harmann · Master of Geoscience, GIS & Technology emphasis · May 2026

Read the thesis ↓ Open PDF in new tab ↗ Download PDF Back to portfolio

Abstract

This study examines how water-related vulnerability is distributed across census tracts in El Paso County, Texas, using a GIS-based framework that brings together social, hydraulic, and environmental data. The region is a useful case study because of its arid climate, reliance on groundwater, and location along the U.S.–Mexico border, where rapid population growth and infrastructure limitations intersect with environmental stress.

Four indices were constructed: a Social Vulnerability Index, a Hydrologic Exposure Index, a Water Vulnerability Index, and an Environmental Pressure Index. All variables were standardized and combined using a weighted approach to allow direct comparison across tracts. Global Moran's I confirmed strong spatial clustering for every index, with the highest vulnerability concentrated in urban core tracts near the border where social disadvantage and environmental exposure overlap.

Four indices

SVISocial Vulnerability Index: median household income (inverted), population density, Hispanic population proportion
HEIHydrologic Exposure Index: FEMA flood exposure, river proximity, groundwater well density
WVIWater Vulnerability Index: weighted combination of SVI and HEI
EPIEnvironmental Pressure Index: NLCD land cover risk and inverted elevation from a DEM

Methods & findings

  • Min–max normalization and weighted combination of census, FEMA, TWDB, NLCD, USGS elevation, and hydrography data at the census-tract scale.
  • Global Moran's I (z-scores of roughly 36.6 to 42.9, p < 0.001) and Getis-Ord Gi* hot spot analysis identified significant clusters of high and low vulnerability.
  • Hot spots concentrate in the central and southeastern urban areas along the river; cold spots appear in the northern and outer parts of the county.
  • A WVI − SVI difference analysis shows social conditions acting as the baseline of vulnerability, with hydrologic exposure amplifying risk in specific flood-prone and river-adjacent locations.
  • Findings support differentiated planning: broad social investment where social vulnerability dominates, and targeted flood or groundwater management where hydrologic exposure matters most.
